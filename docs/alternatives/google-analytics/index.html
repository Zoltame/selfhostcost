Which one to pick

Umami 38,748 stars

Next.js application against PostgreSQL or MySQL. The lightest credible analytics stack: two containers and no separate column store.

At 10 people it needs 1.3 GB of memory and 10.5 GB of disk, which fits the CX23 (cost-optimized) at Hetzner Cloud for $6.70 a month. Setting it up is roughly 2 hours of work, then 0.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Full Google Analytics 4 versus Umami breakdown · Umami at every team size

Plausible Analytics 29,036 stars

Ships as Elixir plus ClickHouse plus PostgreSQL. ClickHouse sets the memory floor, so this stack does not usefully shrink below 2 GB. Disk scales with pageviews rather than with seats.

At 10 people it needs 2.6 GB of memory and 21 GB of disk, which fits the CX23 (cost-optimized) at Hetzner Cloud for $6.70 a month. Setting it up is roughly 6 hours of work, then 1.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Full Google Analytics 4 versus Plausible Analytics breakdown · Plausible Analytics at every team size

Matomo 21,859 stars

The most feature-complete option and the heaviest. Report archiving is the expensive part and wants a cron job with real memory behind it.

At 10 people it needs 2.6 GB of memory and 26 GB of disk, which fits the CX23 (cost-optimized) at Hetzner Cloud for $6.70 a month. Setting it up is roughly 6 hours of work, then 1.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Full Google Analytics 4 versus Matomo breakdown · Matomo at every team size

PostHog 39,757 stars

Kafka, ClickHouse, PostgreSQL, Redis and several workers. PostHog itself now discourages self-hosting below a substantial scale, and the sizing here reflects that honestly.

At 10 people it needs 10.2 GB of memory and 62 GB of disk, which fits the CX43 (cost-optimized) at Hetzner Cloud for $18.46 a month. Setting it up is roughly 16 hours of work, then 3 hours a month to keep it healthy.

    Questions people actually ask

    Is there a free open-source alternative to Google Analytics 4?

    There are 4 on this page. 3 of them are open source, published under AGPL-3.0, GPL-3.0, MIT, with no licence to buy. PostHog ships its source under its own licence rather than a standard open-source one, so check its terms before commercial use. None of them is free to run: the cheapest, Umami, costs $45.54 a month at 10 people once the server, backups and the time to look after it are counted.

    How much does Google Analytics 4 cost?

    Google Analytics 4 Free costs $0.00 a month at 10 people: free at the standard tier. That figure was read from the vendor's own pricing page on 2026-09-12.

    What does it cost to self-host Umami?

    $45.54 a month at 10 people. That is $6.70 for the CX23 (cost-optimized) at Hetzner Cloud, plus backups, plus 0.5 hours a month of your own time priced at the published hourly rate.

    How long before Umami is cheaper than Google Analytics 4?

    never, counting $150 of first-time setup. Over 36 months the difference is $1,789.

    Which Google Analytics 4 alternative is the easiest to run?
