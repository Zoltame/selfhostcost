Which one to pick

Umami 38,748 stars

Next.js application against PostgreSQL or MySQL. The lightest credible analytics stack: two containers and no separate column store.

At 10 people it needs 1.3 GB of memory and 10.5 GB of disk, which fits the Basic 2 GiB at DigitalOcean for $12.00 a month. Setting it up is roughly 2 hours of work, then 0.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Plausible Analytics 29,036 stars

Ships as Elixir plus ClickHouse plus PostgreSQL. ClickHouse sets the memory floor, so this stack does not usefully shrink below 2 GB. Disk scales with pageviews rather than with seats.

At 10 people it needs 2.6 GB of memory and 21 GB of disk, which fits the Basic 4 GiB at DigitalOcean for $24.00 a month. Setting it up is roughly 6 hours of work, then 1.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Full Google Analytics 4 versus Plausible Analytics breakdown · Plausible Analytics at every team size

Matomo 21,859 stars

The most feature-complete option and the heaviest. Report archiving is the expensive part and wants a cron job with real memory behind it.

At 10 people it needs 2.6 GB of memory and 26 GB of disk, which fits the Basic 4 GiB at DigitalOcean for $24.00 a month. Setting it up is roughly 6 hours of work, then 1.5 hours a month to keep it healthy.

Full Google Analytics 4 versus Matomo breakdown · Matomo at every team size

PostHog 39,757 stars

Kafka, ClickHouse, PostgreSQL, Redis and several workers. PostHog itself now discourages self-hosting below a substantial scale, and the sizing here reflects that honestly.

At 10 people it needs 10.2 GB of memory and 62 GB of disk, which fits the Basic 16 GiB at DigitalOcean for $96.00 a month. Setting it up is roughly 16 hours of work, then 3 hours a month to keep it healthy.
