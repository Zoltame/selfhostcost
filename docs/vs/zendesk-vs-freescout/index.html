Zendesk on the Suite Team tier against FreeScout running on the smallest server that fits it. Every figure below is computed from the sizing model and the dated prices at the foot of this page, and includes the part most comparisons leave out: your own time.

At 10 people, self-hosting FreeScout costs $51.90 a month against $638 for Zendesk. You are ahead after under a month and $20,934 better off over 36 months, with your own time already priced in.

What FreeScout needs to run at 10 people

Laravel and MySQL. A shared mailbox rather than a full support suite, which is exactly what most small teams are overpaying Zendesk for.

ResourceRequiredHow it is derived
Memory1.4 GB 1 GB for the stack at rest, plus 8 MB per active user, plus 25% headroom
Disk13 GB 10 GB base, plus 0.3 GB per user
vCPU1 The project's own baseline. For this workload memory runs out before the processor does.

These base figures are an estimate. FreeScout does not publish a minimum specification we could cite, so the base memory and disk are derived from the services in its own reference Docker Compose file. Treat them as a starting point and watch the first week of metrics.

What you give up, stated plainly

  • Uptime is now yours. Zendesk has an operations team on call. Self-hosted, a failed upgrade at 18:00 on a Friday is your evening.
  • Backups only exist if you test restoring them. The $2.40 line above buys snapshots, not a rehearsed recovery.
  • FreeScout is a light lift: one container and no scheduled work beyond image updates. That is why the maintenance line is 0.5 hours a month rather than zero.
  • Feature parity is never exact. FreeScout covers the core of what teams use Zendesk for; the long tail of integrations and enterprise controls is where the gap shows.

Where to put FreeScout

1.4 GB of memory and 13 GB of disk is what this needs at 10 people. The cheapest plan that fits, among the providers whose prices we have read ourselves, is the Basic 2 GiB at DigitalOcean for $12.00 a month.
