Confluence vs self-hosted BookStack: what each actually costs

Confluence on the Standard tier against BookStack running on the smallest server that fits it. Every figure below is computed from the sizing model and the dated prices at the foot of this page, and includes the part most comparisons leave out: your own time.

There is no bill to beat here. Confluence costs nothing at 10 people, so running BookStack yourself costs you $51.90 a month more, not less. The reason to do it is who holds the data, not what it costs.

What BookStack needs to run at 10 people

Laravel and MySQL. The least demanding wiki on this list and the easiest to back up, because it is one database and one uploads directory.

What you give up, stated plainly

  • Uptime is now yours. Confluence has an operations team on call. Self-hosted, a failed upgrade at 18:00 on a Friday is your evening.
  • Backups only exist if you test restoring them. The $2.40 line above buys snapshots, not a rehearsed recovery.
  • BookStack is a light lift: one container and no scheduled work beyond image updates. That is why the maintenance line is 0.5 hours a month rather than zero.
  • Feature parity is never exact. BookStack covers the core of what teams use Confluence for; the long tail of integrations and enterprise controls is where the gap shows.

Where to put BookStack

1.3 GB of memory and 12 GB of disk is what this needs at 10 people. The cheapest plan that fits, among the providers whose prices we have read ourselves, is the Basic 2 GiB at DigitalOcean for $12.00 a month.
