What Vaultwarden needs to run at 10 people

A Rust reimplementation of the Bitwarden server. It runs comfortably in a fraction of a gigabyte because vault data is small and encrypted client-side.

ResourceRequiredHow it is derived
Memory0.3 GB 0.2 GB for the stack at rest, plus 1 MB per active user, plus 25% headroom
Disk2.2 GB 2 GB base, plus 0.02 GB per user
vCPU1 The project's own baseline. For this workload memory runs out before the processor does.

What you give up, stated plainly

  • Uptime is now yours. 1Password has an operations team on call. Self-hosted, a failed upgrade at 18:00 on a Friday is your evening.
  • Backups only exist if you test restoring them. The $0.80 line above buys snapshots, not a rehearsed recovery.
  • Vaultwarden is a light lift: one container and no scheduled work beyond image updates. That is why the maintenance line is 0.5 hours a month rather than zero.
  • Feature parity is never exact. Vaultwarden covers the core of what teams use 1Password for; the long tail of integrations and enterprise controls is where the gap shows.

Where to put Vaultwarden

0.3 GB of memory and 2.2 GB of disk is what this needs at 10 people. The cheapest plan that fits, among the providers whose prices we have read ourselves, is the Basic 512 MiB at DigitalOcean for $4.00 a month.
