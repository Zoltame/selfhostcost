Better Stack Uptime vs self-hosted Uptime Kuma: what each actually costs

Better Stack Uptime on the Responder tier against Uptime Kuma running on the smallest server that fits it. Every figure below is computed from the sizing model and the dated prices at the foot of this page, and includes the part most comparisons leave out: your own time.

At 10 people this one is close enough to go either way. The server costs $4.80 a month against $34.00 for Better Stack Uptime, but add 0.5 hours a month of your attention and self-hosting lands at $42.30 and loses. It only wins if your time is genuinely free.

Read this before you price anything

Better Stack's free tier includes 10 monitors and heartbeats and 1 status page. If that covers you, there is nothing to save.

What Uptime Kuma needs to run at 10 people

One container with SQLite. Cost scales with the number of monitors and their check interval, not with the number of people reading the dashboard.

ResourceRequiredHow it is derived
Memory0.6 GB 0.5 GB for the stack at rest, plus 1 MB per active user, plus 25% headroom

vCPU1 The project's own baseline. For this workload memory runs out before the processor does.

These base figures are an estimate. Uptime Kuma does not publish a minimum specification we could cite, so the base memory and disk are derived from the services in its own reference Docker Compose file. Treat them as a starting point and watch the first week of metrics.

What you give up, stated plainly

  • Uptime is now yours. Better Stack Uptime has an operations team on call. Self-hosted, a failed upgrade at 18:00 on a Friday is your evening.
  • Backups only exist if you test restoring them. The $0.80 line above buys snapshots, not a rehearsed recovery.
  • Uptime Kuma is a light lift: one container and no scheduled work beyond image updates. That is why the maintenance line is 0.5 hours a month rather than zero.
  • Feature parity is never exact. Uptime Kuma covers the core of what teams use Better Stack Uptime for; the long tail of integrations and enterprise controls is where the gap shows.

We are not going to tell you to self-host this

At 10 people, Better Stack Uptime wins on cost once your time is priced at $75 an hour. Self-hosting Uptime Kuma here only makes sense for reasons that are not on the invoice: data residency, avoiding a vendor, or wanting to run the thing yourself. Those are real reasons. Saving money is not one of them at this size.

Where to put Uptime Kuma

0.6 GB of memory and 5.1 GB of disk is what this needs at 10 people. The cheapest plan that fits, among the providers whose prices we have read ourselves, is the Basic at Kamatera for $4.00 a month.
